# At-bat flow UX review (Sept 2026)

Status: **recommendation, not yet agreed.** Produced 2026-09-14 from a five-lens review of the CTG charting screen (each finding checked by two adversarial verifiers), three independent redesign proposals scored by three judges, and a synthesis. Nothing here changes `PRODUCT_SPEC.md` until Joe picks a direction; the phases below are the proposed order of work. The same review, with an interactive mockup, is published as the "At-Bat Flow Review" artifact.

## Brief (Joe, verbatim)

> I've noticed that as I navigate the at-bat experience, the UI feels clunky and it's hard for me to go back and forth between at-bats, which I should be able to do as I rethink about each play. I should also be able to just choose win or loss without an explanation or clicking a specific type of W or L. Maybe the W and L themselves are more general buttons.

## Headline

Build the Capture Dock: a fixed bottom dock where the big L and W record a plain at-bat in one tap with the twelve types collapsible directly beneath them, over the prototype's batting-order list where every at-bat is a tappable tile that re-judges in place, with the Tape's back/forward steppers and the Scorebook's snap-back, backfill and delete rules grafted on.

## Diagnosis

The screen has exactly one way to go backwards, and it is the wrong one. The store exposes recordAtBat and undoLastAtBat and nothing else (lib/store.tsx:81-117, 511-539); undo always deletes the newest at-bat in the game and drags inning/half back to it. There is no updateAtBat or deleteAtBat, the scorebook on the Stats tab has no Pressable anywhere, and the natural "fix that call" gesture, tapping a charted batter's row, is wired to skipTo, so it asks "Skip 9 batters?" and then appends a duplicate at-bat that the row hides (F01, F02). Fixing the batter two back is six taps plus recalling the intervening outcomes; fixing a previous half-inning an inning later is about sixteen and nobody does it, so wrong calls stay for the season. Undo tapped right after Next half silently deletes the last at-bat of the previous half and flips the clock (F06). That is the whole of "hard to go back and forth": the model has no addressable at-bat, so every correction is destruction plus re-entry from memory.

The capture surface turns a one-bit judgment into a twelve-way read. The big L and W are the largest type on the screen and sit exactly where a primary button would, but they are inert Text (index.tsx:279-281), and recordAtBat requires one of the twelve ids, so a plain W or L cannot be recorded (F12). The twelve have no home for a routine ground out, pop-up or HBP, so the coach taps "Fielder's Choice, Weak Hit Ball" to get the L in and the season scorebook records a lie (F03); that pressure is where "just choose win or loss" comes from. All twelve Pressables are identical 145x38 tiles so nothing is primary, and a second tap during the transition charts the next batter with the same outcome because there is no lockout and the grid re-renders at the same pixel position (F11).

What just happened then disappears. The auto-scroll gives the grid, ON DECK and IN THE HOLE priority over the previous row, so the 36px W or L the coach wants to glance at is behind the inning strip and the only trace is a 12px, 2.6:1 gray caption that never says W or L (F04); on a 375x667 phone the Hit row lays out under the End Game footer (F16); the list shows one result per batter for the current half only, so batting around overwrites, earlier innings vanish, and the only way back (Prev half) leaves the twelve buttons live in a past half (F07). The coach cannot verify, cannot revisit, and is nudged to mislabel. Every one of these is a structural gap, not a polish item, and every one is fixed by the same two moves: make the at-bat the addressable unit, and make the L and W the controls.

## Principles

- One tap records; the twelve refine. The big L and W are the primary controls; a type is never required and can be attached to any at-bat later.
- Nothing the coach can see is inert. Every displayed result (row tile, mini chip, LAST readout, scorebook cell) opens the same edit path, and a fix changes that at-bat only: never the batter pointer, the game clock, or anything recorded after it.
- The record controls never move. The dock is fixed above the tab bar, so the batter due up, the last result and the L/W buttons are on screen without scrolling on every phone from 375x667 up.
- Recording is quiet, correcting is loud. A record is one tap, one needle swing, one haptic, then a 450ms lockout; re-judge mode is orange, names the at-bat, and clears itself after every commit so the next tap always charts the batter who is actually up.
- Undo reverses the last thing you did, by name. Record, skip, half change, re-judge and remove go on one stack; the button says what it will undo and never crosses a half-inning silently.
- Keep the owner's screen. The numbered list with 20pt names, the AT-BAT / ON DECK / IN THE HOLE tags, the outcome under the name, the gauge between the L and the W, and the 2x6 red/green grid keep their places; what changes is what they do, not how they look.

## Recommendation: Capture Dock, grafted

Take Capture Dock as the base because it is the only proposal that keeps the owner's list as the screen, puts the L/W buttons where the thumb is, keeps the main surface always live (mode errors are bounded), and is the cheapest to build. Fix its two real weaknesses with grafts: the twelve types move out of a modal and into the dock as a collapsible grid under the L/gauge/W row (Live Scorebook), so typed capture stays one tap and the signature grid stays visible; and correction gets an in-place fast path (tap a tile, tap a type, snap back to live) plus back/forward steppers (Tape and Card) instead of open-sheet/close-sheet per at-bat. The route-modal editor stays for the rare fields (pitcher, half, batter, remove) and as the landing from a Stats scorebook cell, so there is one full editor and one fast path that share OutcomeButtons. The judges' tally was a three-way tie (74/73/73 once the split name keys are summed; each judge picked a different winner), so the choice rests on the constraints: keep the list, keep the twelve one tap away, lowest RN Web risk. Live Scorebook's grid is not adopted as the charting screen but its best mechanics are, and the read-only scorebook on Stats becomes the second door into the same editor.

### Capture flow

Plain at-bat (the default, 1 tap): the coach taps the big W. recordAtBat(game.id, 'plain_w') appends an at-bat with outcomeId 'plain_w' and result 'W' and advances the pointer. In order: the W button shows its pressed state and a white check for 400ms; expo-haptics light impact (no-op on web); the needle swings to W and eases back as today; the LAST readout becomes 'Cooper W. · W · add type ›'; Cooper's row gains a solid W tile and a W mini chip and the list scrolls so that row sits directly above Carsyn's orange-railed AT-BAT row; the dock header reads '5. Carsyn Griffith (#26)' with her chips; the Undo label reads 'Undo W'. All fourteen buttons ignore presses until 450ms after the record (a lockedUntil timestamp checked in onPress; the lockout is set after any confirmAction resolves, never before, and never blocks Undo, the steppers or the strip). A second tap inside the window does nothing. A second tap after it visibly records for the next batter: the LAST readout names Ryder under Carsyn's header and Ryder's row carries a tile, so the phantom is seen, not inferred, and Undo is one tap.

A 6-3 ground out, a pop-up, an HBP: one honest tap on L or W, no false type.

Typed at-bat: with the types open, tapping 'Hit' records 'hit' in one tap exactly as today (result derived from the type). With the types collapsed it is two taps once (toggle, then Hit) and they stay open. Nothing about the twelve changes: same component, same two columns, same order, labels, colors and pitcher-perspective flip.

Refining afterwards (2 taps, any time in the game): tap the LAST readout's 'add type ›' (or the at-bat's tile, or a mini chip), which puts the dock in re-judge mode for that at-bat, then tap the type. The dock commits and snaps back to live. This is how 'plain W now, Sac Fly when I have a second' works, and it never touches the batter pointer.

How a plain at-bat looks versus a typed one: tile letter alone versus letter over the short code; LAST readout 'Cooper W. · W' versus '· Fly Out, Hard Hit Ball'; scorebook cell big letter alone versus letter over the code; season tables, game HITTING/PITCHING lines and lineup ranking are W/L only and identical for both.

Pitching half: the left big button is the green W (our pitcher won), the right is the red L; pressing the left W records 'plain_l' because AtBat.result stays the batter's. With no pitcher set the buttons are disabled at 40% behind the header's orange 'Set pitcher', which opens the pitcher sheet in place; picking one calls setPitcher (which already credits this half's unassigned at-bats) and re-enables the buttons: two taps on one screen instead of four across three.

Bat-around: when the pointer returns to a batter who already has an at-bat this half, the dock stays in chart mode (a new tap appends a second at-bat; the row's tile shows the newest, the mini chips show both, the scorebook cell stacks both) and his earlier chip is visible in the dock header for re-judging instead.

The 'W then type' trap (tap W, then tap Hit meaning to refine, and record two at-bats) is closed by the explicit 'add type ›' path being visible in the LAST readout immediately after every plain record. An implicit refine window is deliberately not shipped in the first pass (open question 3).

### Moving between at-bats

Where am I: four readouts that always agree without scrolling: the strip ('▲ 2nd', the solid pill, the pitcher chip or the amber review line), the dock header (who is AT-BAT and his game so far), the LAST readout (the newest at-bat, with '· ▲ 1st' when it belongs to an earlier half), and the dock's mode border (blue charting, orange re-judging). In the list, the orange rail marks the batter due up, solid tiles are this half's results, mini chips are the whole game.

Between at-bats, four ways, all reaching the same selection: (1) the ‹ › steppers in the dock: ‹ from live selects the newest at-bat, ‹ again the one before, › moves forward, › past the newest returns to live. They walk at-bats in inning, half, recordedAt order (the same sort every view uses, so a backfilled at-bat sits in its inning). (2) Tap a tile or mini chip on a row. (3) Tap the LAST readout. (4) Tap a cell on the Stats scorebook, which opens the full editor for that at-bat (a multi-cell opens the batter sheet at that inning). Every batter's row carries his whole game as chips, so the 5th batter's two earlier at-bats are on his row at the moment the coach thinks about pinch-hitting for him, and tapping the row body opens the batter sheet: a newest-first list of his at-bats this game ('▲ 2nd · Fly Out, Hard Hit Ball · W tile', each opening the editor) with 'Bring up to bat now' when his side is batting.

Selecting an at-bat: the tile gets a 3pt orange ring, the list scrolls that row into view, the dock turns orange and names the at-bat; if the at-bat belongs to the side that is not batting (fixing our 1st-inning K during their 2nd), the list peeks to that side's order for as long as the selection lasts and the strip's line 2 shows the amber review line. The selection clears (back to live, list returns to the batting side and rescrolls to the current batter) on: any commit, the '▶ Now' chip, Undo, Next half, Prev half, End Game, or the screen losing focus. There is no way to record while an at-bat is selected, so a stray tap after a peek can only change the ringed at-bat that is on screen.

Between half-innings: Next half and Prev half stay on the strip, cost one tap, and both push an entry on the undo stack, so Undo right after Next half reads 'Undo Next half' and deletes nothing. Prev half exists only to genuinely rewind the clock; looking at old at-bats never requires it. When the clock is behind the newest charted half the amber review line persists (no timed notice) with 'Jump ahead' stepping nextHalfInning to the newest half with at-bats; the L/W buttons stay live there so a forgotten at-bat can still be charted, and the dock's AT-BAT label reads 'AT-BAT · ▲ 1st' in amber.

Skipping a batter: only the forward-step target on a row or 'Bring up to bat now' in the batter sheet. No dialog for the next batter; 'Bring Lucas Kloster (#13) up now? 2 batters will be skipped' for a forward skip, counting only batters without a result this half; 'Lucas Kloster (#13) already batted this half. Give him another at-bat now? 8 batters will be skipped.' for a batter who has; never a backward skip. A skip pushes {kind: 'skip', side, fromBatterId, toBatterId} on the stack; Undo reads 'Undo skip'. Undo entries store batter ids, resolved through the current order at undo time, and are dropped if the batter has left the order.

### Re-judging an at-bat

Fast path, on the CTG screen (2 taps, nothing re-entered): tap the tile (dock: orange border, 'RE-JUDGE · Ryder B. · ▲ 2nd · L Fielder's Choice, Weak Hit Ball', the type ringed in the grid, the recorded big letter solid and the other outlined), then one of: tap the outlined big letter, which calls updateAtBat(id, { outcomeId: plainFor(newResult) }) (a type from the other side cannot survive a flip, so the at-bat becomes plain); tap any type, which sets that outcome and its derived result; tap the ringed type again, which drops it to plain with the same result; tap the solid big letter, which changes nothing. Every commit swings the needle, flashes the tile with a 15% tint of its result color for one second, buzzes with a distinct (double) haptic, pushes {kind: 're-judge', atBatId, previous fields} on the stack, and returns the dock to live. Batter pointer, inning, half, score and every later at-bat are untouched. Cost: the batter two back is tile + type (was 6 taps plus memory); the previous at-bat is LAST + type (was Undo + type, destructive); a 2nd-inning K found in the 4th is Stats cell + type or ‹ ‹ ‹ + type (was about 16).

## Alternatives considered

- **Tape and Card**: Its L/W buttons sit mid-screen inside the scroll region rather than at the thumb, its height budget is exact at 812 with the order list under the fold, it adds a new horizontal element (the tape) to a list-centric design, and nothing returns the cursor to live after an edit, so a coach who looks up at the field and back can re-judge instead of record. - **Live Scorebook**: It replaces the owner's numbered list, which is the CTG screen's visual identity in the 2024 design, with a grid of 15px names, 10px tags and 8px codes, which is worse in glare; it is the riskiest build in this codebase (a three-pinned-column interactive grid with a sticky header and nested horizontal scroll on React Native Web and mobile Safari, long-press on name cells, four dock modes); and all three judges scored it 2 on cost. Its dock, its snap-back selection model, backfill, delete rule and End Game placement are grafted; the scorebook itself stays on the Stats tab as the second door into the same editor.
- **Capture Dock as proposed**: The twelve types leave the charting screen behind a 'Play type' chip and a modal, so typed capture regresses from one tap to two plus an animation and the signature red/green grid disappears, which every judge scored down on preservation; End Game sinks to the end of the list; there is no way to step between at-bats; sheet edits are not undoable; and an our-side fix during their half needs Stats or Prev half. - **Stop after Phase 2 (plain W/L on the existing letters plus an editor, no layout change)**: It ships both of the owner's asks cheaply, and it is a legitimate checkpoint, but it leaves the previous result hidden behind the strip after every record, the bottom button row under the footer on 375x667, and the L/W buttons mid-scroll, so the 'clunky' half of the brief stays.
